Listening to the Planet

The central idea behind the project is simple. Information about the state of the planet is conveyed almost exclusively through images—drought maps, temperature anomalies, time series, and satellite images—which are currently, for the most part, inaccessible to people with visual impairments. An “Earth jukebox,” which converts observational data—such as temperature, precipitation, snow, humidity, and many other factors—into sound synchronized with its visual representation, opens up a second channel of access: the ear.

NASA has already explored this path with its Universe of Sound program, which translates data from space telescopes into sound. A study published in 2024 in Frontiers in Communication surveyed 3,184 people—both sighted and visually impaired—and found:

  • Significant improvements in participants’ perceived learning.
  • Positive responses regarding enjoyment of the experience.
  • That engaging multiple senses creates new avenues of trust in scientific data, broadens access, and raises awareness about accessibility among both the sighted public and people who are blind or have low vision.
